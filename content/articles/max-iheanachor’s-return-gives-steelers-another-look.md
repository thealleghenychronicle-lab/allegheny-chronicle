# Max Iheanachor’s Return Gives Steelers One More Look at Their First-Round Investment

**PITTSBURGH —** The Pittsburgh Steelers are getting one of their most important young offensive linemen back at a critical point in the preseason.

First-round draft pick Max Iheanachor returned to practice Monday after missing more than two weeks with a shoulder and neck injury, putting the rookie offensive tackle in position to potentially make his preseason debut Thursday against the Buffalo Bills.

Iheanachor told reporters after Monday's practice that the plan is for him to play in the Steelers' preseason finale. He has not appeared in either of Pittsburgh's first two preseason games.

[Source: WPXI — August 25, 2026](https://www.wpxi.com/news/local/steelers-first-round-pick-plans-make-debut-vs-bills/SRKXSXL3CZDJBEUGSPM7YNHLKI/)

## A First-Round Player Finally Gets on the Field

The Steelers selected Iheanachor with the 21st overall pick in April's NFL Draft, making him the team's first selection of the 2026 class.

The Arizona State product was brought in to strengthen an offensive line that Pittsburgh has been working to improve, particularly on the right side.

The rookie's development was temporarily interrupted when he left a training-camp practice on Aug. 6 after suffering what initially appeared to be an upper-body injury.

Pittsburgh head coach Mike McCarthy said at the time that Iheanachor was expected to be OK, although the injury ultimately kept the rookie out of practice for substantially longer than initially anticipated.

[Source: Pittsburgh Steelers — August 7, 2026](https://www.steelers.com/news/iheanachor-expected-to-be-ok)

That lost time matters for a first-round pick. Iheanachor entered training camp competing with veterans and other young offensive linemen while learning a new professional system.

His return now gives the Steelers a limited opportunity to evaluate him in game conditions before the regular season begins.

## The Timing Is Important

Thursday's preseason finale against Buffalo is particularly valuable because Pittsburgh is still working through several offensive-line questions.

The *Pittsburgh Post-Gazette* reported Wednesday that starting jobs and roster spots remain unsettled heading into the final preseason game, including competition involving the offensive line.

[Source: Pittsburgh Post-Gazette — August 26, 2026](https://www.post-gazette.com/sports/steelers/2026/08/26/steelers-preseason-bills-dylan-cook-max-iheanachor-allar-howard/stories/202608260033)

Iheanachor's return gives the Steelers another piece of information before the organization has to finalize its roster.

That makes Thursday's game about more than simply getting a rookie some preseason snaps. It gives Pittsburgh an opportunity to see how its first-round investment performs against NFL competition after spending most of August watching from the sideline.

## Pittsburgh Has Already Invested in Iheanachor

The Steelers' commitment to Iheanachor began with the 21st overall selection and continued when the team signed him to a four-year contract in May.

At Arizona State, Iheanachor appeared in 32 games and made 31 starts. The Steelers also noted that he did not allow a sack during his senior season and earned second-team All-Big 12 honors in 2025.

[Source: Pittsburgh Steelers — May 15, 2026](https://www.steelers.com/news/steelers-sign-iheanachor)

Those credentials explain why his availability matters.

The Steelers did not draft Iheanachor simply as developmental depth. A first-round selection represents a significant investment in a player's potential, and the organization needs to determine how quickly that investment can contribute.

## A Short Evaluation Window

Iheanachor's return comes with an obvious limitation: he has missed valuable practice and preseason time.

He has not played in Pittsburgh's first two preseason games, meaning Thursday could be his first opportunity to experience an NFL game in a Steelers uniform.

That makes expectations difficult to measure. The Steelers will have to evaluate his technique, communication and ability to handle the speed of an NFL game while also recognizing that he is returning from an injury and missed much of training camp.

The goal does not necessarily have to be proving that Iheanachor is immediately ready for a full-time starting role.

Instead, Pittsburgh can use the game to determine how close he is to that point.

## The Bigger Roster Question

For the Steelers, Iheanachor's return also adds another variable to an offensive-line group that has already generated plenty of competition during camp.

If the rookie performs well Thursday, Pittsburgh could have more flexibility when deciding how to deploy its tackles once the regular season begins.

If he needs more development, the organization has to determine whether he is better served by easing him into the lineup or continuing with a more experienced option while Iheanachor develops.

Those decisions carry football consequences, but they also affect how efficiently the Steelers can turn a first-round draft investment into production.

With the regular season opener against the Atlanta Falcons scheduled for Sept. 13, the Steelers have less than three weeks to make those decisions.

## One Final Audition

Iheanachor's return does not guarantee that he will start Thursday or open the regular season as Pittsburgh's starting right tackle.

It does, however, give the Steelers something they have been missing throughout the first two preseason games: an opportunity to evaluate their first-round offensive tackle in an actual game.

For Iheanachor, Thursday represents the first chance to turn the positive impressions he created during training camp into game performance.

For the Steelers, it is one of the final opportunities to determine how quickly their latest first-round investment can become part of the team's plans.

**The Allegheny Chronicle** will continue to follow Iheanachor's development and the Steelers' offensive-line competition as the regular season approaches.
