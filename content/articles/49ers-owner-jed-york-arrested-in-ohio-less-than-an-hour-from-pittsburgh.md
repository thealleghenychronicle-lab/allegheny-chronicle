# 49ers Owner Jed York Arrested in Ohio, Less Than an Hour From Pittsburgh

San Francisco 49ers principal owner Jed York was arrested Sunday in East Palestine, Ohio, in an undercover prostitution operation — putting one of the NFL's most prominent owners in trouble just across the Pennsylvania border.

York, 46, was arrested at the Wheat Hill Mobile Home Community in East Palestine, roughly 50 miles northwest of Pittsburgh. The arrest was carried out by East Palestine police with assistance from the Mahoning Valley Human Trafficking Task Force. [CBS News reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/san-francisco-49ers-owner-jed-york-arrested-east-palestine-ohio/) that York was taken into custody at approximately 9:35 a.m. Sunday.

The arrest came just weeks before the start of the 2026 NFL regular season.

## What Led to the Arrest?

According to court documents obtained by ESPN and reported by [ABC News](https://abc7.com/19727825/), York allegedly responded to an advertisement on a prostitution website and arranged to meet a woman for sexual activity in exchange for $140.

Authorities said York arrived at the meeting location with the cellphone used to arrange the encounter and was arrested as part of the operation.

York was initially charged with misdemeanor engaging in prostitution and possessing criminal tools. The prostitution charge was later amended to disorderly conduct as part of a plea agreement.

He posted a $5,000 bond following his arrest.

## York Pleads No Contest

The case moved quickly.

On Monday, York pleaded no contest to misdemeanor charges of disorderly conduct and possessing criminal tools. According to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/sports/reports-49ers-owner-jed-york-arrested-ohio-over-weekend--flm-2026-08-24/), the criminal-tools charge involved the cellphone allegedly used to arrange the encounter.

York was sentenced to one day in jail on each charge, with the sentences running concurrently. He received credit for time already served and was ordered to pay $1,150 in fines.

The original prostitution charge was therefore not the charge to which York ultimately pleaded.

The 49ers issued a brief statement following the case.

> "As this is a legal matter, which has been resolved, we will not be providing any further comment at this time."

## An Arrest Close to York's Hometown

The location of the arrest is particularly notable because East Palestine is only about 20 miles from Youngstown, where York was born and raised.

York has been closely associated with the 49ers organization for years. He became the team's CEO in 2008 and became its principal owner in 2024 after acquiring additional ownership from his family.

Under York's leadership, the 49ers have become one of the NFL's most successful franchises, including multiple NFC Championship appearances and Super Bowl trips.

That football success now takes a back seat to an incident that occurred much closer to York's Ohio roots than to the team's home in California.

## The NFL Says It Will Review the Matter

The legal case in Ohio has been resolved, but York could still face scrutiny from the NFL.

The league said it is aware of the incident and will review it under the NFL's personal conduct policy, according to [ESPN's report](https://abc7.com/19727825/).

It remains unclear whether the league will impose any discipline on York.

For the 49ers, the timing is especially notable. The team is approaching the start of another NFL season, and York remains the franchise's principal owner and one of its most important decision-makers.

For Pittsburgh and Western Pennsylvania, meanwhile, the story has an unusually close-to-home connection. The owner of an NFL team that normally operates nearly 3,000 miles away was arrested in a small Ohio community less than an hour from the Pittsburgh region.

York's Ohio arrest and subsequent no-contest plea have already resolved the criminal case, but the NFL's review could determine whether the matter has further consequences for the 49ers owner.
