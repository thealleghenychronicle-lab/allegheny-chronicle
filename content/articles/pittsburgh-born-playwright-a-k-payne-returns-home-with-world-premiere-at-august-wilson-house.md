# Pittsburgh-Born Playwright a.k. payne Returns Home With World Premiere at August Wilson House

A Pittsburgh playwright who has quickly become one of the most recognized young voices in American theater is returning home this week for a major milestone: the world premiere of a new play rooted deeply in the city where they grew up.

a.k. payne's **"BURNBABYBURN! the american dream"** begins its Pittsburgh run Tuesday, August 25, at the [August Wilson House](https://augustwilsonhouse.org/) in the city's Hill District. The production is a collaboration between [Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre Company](https://www.pghplaywrights.org/) and the August Wilson House.

The production runs through September 13 and represents the first professional staging of a full-length work by payne in their hometown.

According to [90.5 WESA's report on the production](https://www.wesa.fm/arts-culture/2026-08-25/ak-payne-playwright), the play is especially personal for payne because its story is closely connected to Pittsburgh, its neighborhoods and the generations of people who have shaped the city.

## A Story Rooted in Pittsburgh

"BURNBABYBURN! the american dream" follows Sky II, a young woman attempting to understand her family history and find a way forward by connecting with the generations who came before her.

The story moves between different periods and locations, including Pittsburgh's Hill District and Homewood neighborhoods, as Sky II confronts questions involving family, love, loss, motherhood and identity.

The [official production description from Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre](https://www.pghplaywrights.org/season-info/burn/) describes the play as a journey in which Sky II uses photographs and memories to reach across generations and confront the experiences of her ancestors.

Rather than telling the story in a straightforward chronological fashion, the production moves through time, allowing different generations to exist alongside one another.

That approach gives the play a particularly Pittsburgh-centered perspective while also exploring themes that extend well beyond the city.

## A Homecoming for a Rising Theater Voice

Payne's return to Pittsburgh comes after several years of growing national recognition.

Their earlier play, **"Furlough's Paradise,"** premiered in Atlanta in 2024 before receiving productions at theaters around the country. The play also reached Yale Repertory Theatre and the Geffen Playhouse in Los Angeles.

Payne received the prestigious **Susan Smith Blackburn Prize in 2025** for "Furlough's Paradise," adding to a growing list of national honors.

Earlier this summer, payne was also named **Best Playwright at the 2026 NAACP Theatre Awards**, according to [OnStage Pittsburgh](https://onstagepittsburgh.com/2026/06/30/pittsburghs-a-k-payne-named-best-playwright-at-naacp-theatre-awards/).

That recognition has helped establish payne as a significant emerging voice in American theater, making the decision to premiere their latest work in Pittsburgh especially notable.

## From Mount Oliver to the National Stage

Payne's connection to Pittsburgh goes back well before the recent national attention.

[WESA reports](https://www.wesa.fm/arts-culture/2026-08-25/ak-payne-playwright) that payne grew up in Mount Oliver and became involved in the performing arts at a young age. Their early experiences included local choirs, Pittsburgh Opera and training through the Pittsburgh CLO Academy and Alumni Theatre Company.

They eventually discovered playwriting while attending Pittsburgh CAPA, where writing plays became a way to explore stories and bring people together.

Payne's first play to reach a stage was "To Pave Her Path," a one-act drama about a young girl who wants to become a civil rights activist. It was selected for City Theatre's Young Playwrights Festival in 2010.

Years later, payne would study at Yale, earning an undergraduate degree before completing a master's degree at the Yale School of Drama.

The path from a Pittsburgh student discovering playwriting to an award-winning playwright working with theaters across the country makes the new production feel like a particularly meaningful return home.

## Four Generations, One Pittsburgh Story

"BURNBABYBURN!" centers on four generations of women and explores how family history can continue influencing people long after the events themselves have passed.

The production moves between decades and locations, with the Hill District and Homewood serving as important parts of its world.

The [official Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre listing](https://www.pghplaywrights.org/season-info/burn/) notes that the story examines motherhood, love, loss and the complicated relationship between people and the places they call home.

For payne, Pittsburgh itself is more than simply a backdrop.

The city's hills, neighborhoods and history have become part of the way they approach storytelling. That connection is particularly appropriate for a production taking place at the August Wilson House, a location associated with one of America's most influential playwrights.

The setting also gives Pittsburgh audiences an opportunity to see a locally rooted story performed in a neighborhood that has played an important role in the city's cultural history.

## An Outdoor Production at the August Wilson House

The world premiere is being presented outdoors in the backyard of the August Wilson House in the Hill District.

[Visit Pittsburgh lists the production](https://www.visitpittsburgh.com/events/burnbabyburn-an-american-dream/) as running from August 25 through September 13, with performances beginning at 7 p.m.

The official production information lists general admission at $35, with a $25 price available for youth age 30 and younger. Artists can also purchase tickets for $25 at the door. 

An indoor matinee has additionally been scheduled for September 12 at the Madison Arts Center, according to [Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre](https://www.pghplaywrights.org/season-info/burn/).

The production is directed by Karla C. Payne, who is not related to the playwright.

## Why the Premiere Matters to Pittsburgh

Pittsburgh has long produced influential artists, musicians, actors and writers, but the city has a particularly deep connection to American theater through the legacy of August Wilson.

Wilson's work famously chronicled Black life in Pittsburgh's Hill District across different decades. Payne's new work approaches Pittsburgh from a different generation and perspective while continuing the tradition of using the city as an important part of the story.

The result is more than simply a local theater production.

It is an opportunity for Pittsburgh audiences to see a playwright who grew up in the region return with a work that has already generated national interest.

As [Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre explains in its production announcement](https://www.pghplaywrights.org/season-info/burn/), the company sees the production as a chance to bring home an artist whose work has increasingly reached audiences around the country.

For Pittsburgh, that makes the timing significant. Payne is not returning home at the beginning of their career. They are returning after establishing themselves on a national stage.

And now, their newest story is being told here.

## "BURNBABYBURN!" Runs Through September 13

"BURNBABYBURN! the american dream" runs **August 25 through September 13, 2026**, outdoors at the August Wilson House in Pittsburgh's Hill District.

The production is a world-premiere collaboration between Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre Company and the August Wilson House.

Tickets and additional performance information are available through the [official Pittsburgh Playwrights Theatre website](https://www.pghplaywrights.org/season-info/burn/burn-tickets/).

For Pittsburgh audiences interested in seeing a rising American playwright tell a story shaped by the city itself, the production offers something unusual: a nationally recognized artist coming home to premiere a work that could only have been created from the perspective of someone who knows Pittsburgh from the inside.
